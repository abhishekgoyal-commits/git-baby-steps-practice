# Delivery Management Reporting Validation Rules

Use these checks before finalizing any sprint, weekly, or project delivery report.

## Validation Checklist

### 1. Status is explicit and evidence-based
- Confirm the report states an overall delivery status as Green, Yellow, or Red.
- Ensure the status is supported by current facts such as sprint progress, blockers, or milestone risk.
- Avoid vague language such as “doing well” or “on track” without measurable context.

### 2. Risks and blockers are clearly identified
- Each material risk must include a clear issue, impact, and owner or escalation path.
- Flag blocked work, dependency delays, decision gaps, and unresolved impediments separately from routine work.
- Confirm the report distinguishes between a risk that is manageable and one that threatens delivery commitments.

### 3. Milestones and targets are realistic and current
- Check that planned milestones, sprint goals, or release dates are stated with dates or windows.
- Confirm progress is measured against the current plan rather than an outdated baseline.
- If a milestone is slipping, document the reason, the expected impact, and the recovery action.

### 4. Dependencies and cross-team impacts are visible
- Capture external dependencies, team handoffs, approval bottlenecks, and vendor or platform constraints.
- Confirm the report names the dependent teams or stakeholders involved.
- Ensure any dependency risk is connected to specific delivery outcomes, not just listed as a general concern.

### 5. Capacity and workload signals are included
- Review whether staffing changes, overload, or reduced availability are reflected in the delivery narrative.
- Confirm workload trends are explained when team capacity has changed or work is being re-prioritized.
- Highlight sustained over-allocation or skill shortages if they affect pace or quality.

### 6. Actions and decisions are clear and time-bound
- Ensure the report lists next actions, owners, and expected timing for the next reporting period.
- Confirm any decisions needed from leadership, stakeholders, or other teams are called out explicitly.
- If no action is needed, state that clearly and explain why the team is stable.

## Quick Pass/Fail Rule
A delivery report passes validation only when it is specific, measurable, risk-aware, and action-oriented. If a report omits owner, impact, or timing, it should be revised before distribution.
